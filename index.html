Sources & how this is calculated ▾
This is a starting point, not a guarantee. Kart tyre pressure has no universal formula — it depends on track grip, surface temperature, kart setup and driver style, and every tyre maker says to confirm hot pressures with a gauge straight off the track. Use these numbers to set your cold fill, then measure hot and log the result so the app (and you) get more accurate for that track over time.
Baseline compound ranges and adjustments were built from:
· MG Tires – Tips
· Nashville Karting Group – MG tyre pressure guide
· TKART – the Vega method for kart tyre pressure
· Vega USA – XM4 & XH4 compound specs
· Flow Racers – go-kart tyre pressure reference
· KartletiX – setting tyre pressures
· Dunlop Kartsport Australia
· Karting Australia – LeCont LH03 AUS dry tyre spec (2022–2026)
· Patrizicorse – Karting Australia 2022–2026 LeCont competition tyre FAQ
· KartSportNews – KA 2025 rule book (Maxxis Sport / 4SS, LeCont / Rotax)
· DWT Racing – M-Series LV magnesium wheel
· Open-Meteo – free weather forecast & historical weather data (the Weather card)
· Karting Queensland – affiliated clubs & track specs
· KartSportNews – Cooloola Coast rebuild report
· KartClass – QLD outdoor track lengths & turn counts
· CRG Factory Race Team / SSC Racing – chassis & tyre setup manual

Bridgestone YDS and the OTK/CRG wheel entries have very little published pressure data, so they're treated as close to the generic baseline — a rougher starting point than the others until you log your own numbers against them.

On left/right split — what's a rule of thumb vs what's real: cornering weight transfers onto the outside tyres, so a track with more right-hand corners loads and heats the left-side tyres harder (and vice versa) — that side gets set 0.15 psi lower cold as a starting-point heuristic. But there is no published, authoritative left/right psi number — the CRG factory manual's own method isn't a formula either: run equal pressures as a baseline, then after a session record each corner's actual hot reading and nudge that corner's own cold fill until all four rise a consistent ~2 psi. That's exactly what the Measured pressures grid in the Session Log is for.

Your own data beats all of this: your sync code (top of the page) carries your session log and remembered track settings to any device you open the same code on. Tap "Remember grip & direction for this track" once you know a track, and log your measured pressures every session — that becomes more accurate than any generic source here.
