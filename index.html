Sources & how this is calculated ▾
This is a starting point, not a guarantee. Kart tyre pressure has no universal formula — it depends on track grip, surface temperature, kart setup and driver style, and every tyre maker says to confirm hot pressures with a gauge straight off the track. Use these numbers to set your cold fill, then measure hot and log the result so the app (and you) get more accurate for that track over time.

Bridgestone YDS has very little published pressure data, so its baseline is conservative — treat it as a rougher starting point than the others.

On turn counts: Ipswich (14 turns), Sandy Creek/Warwick (13) and Gympie Gold/Cooloola (11) are the only QLD club tracks with a published turn count I could find. None of the public sources break that down into which turns are left- vs right-handers — the one guide detailed enough to do that (a third-party Ipswich track guide) is a paid product, so I left it out rather than guess at its contents.

Your own track knowledge beats any of this: tap "Remember grip & direction for this track" after you've dialled in the grip and turn-bias selectors for a track you actually race at, and the app will auto-apply that next time you pick the same track (saved on this device, overriding the generic defaults above). That's the most precise per-track data this app can have — it comes from you, not a search result.

On left/right split — what's a rule of thumb vs what's real: cornering weight transfers onto the outside tyres, so a track with more right-hand corners loads and heats the left-side tyres harder (and vice versa) — that side gets set 0.15 psi lower cold as a starting-point heuristic, in line with the small 0.1–0.2 psi split described by Flow Racers' go-kart tyre pressure guide. But there is no published, authoritative left/right psi number — I checked an actual CRG factory team setup manual (SSC Racing, 2005, still the standard reference many teams use) and its own method isn't a formula either: run equal pressures as a baseline, then after a session record each corner's actual hot reading and nudge that corner's own cold fill up or down until all four rise a consistent ~2 psi from cold to hot. That corner-by-corner method is exactly what the Measured pressures grid in the Session Log above is for — fill it in every session and you'll have better left/right data for your kart, on your tyres, at your track than any generic split could give you.

On track selection: the length/direction/capacity shown for each QLD track come from Karting Queensland's own club database. Actual day-to-day grip level (how rubbered-in a track is) isn't published anywhere searchable for most of these clubs — it lives in local knowledge and closed club Facebook groups. Cooloola Coast is the one track with a citable public signal (a full pavement rebuild, reopened Jan 2025) so it defaults the grip selector to Low; every other track is left on your manual grip choice rather than guessed at. Log your own hot readings per track and this becomes more accurate than any generic source.
